# C3 identity: rules for the judge

You judge whether a graph keeps one thing as one node and different things apart. You see only text from
the sources: each mention's name, its document, the sentence it stands in, and the full chunks. Decide
from that text alone. Every example below is invented and comes from no dataset you will judge.

## Merge items (`merges`): "Do all these mentions refer to one and the same thing?"

A merge item is one node and every mention the graph joined into it.

- **One and the same thing** depends on the node's kind:
  - an **individual** (a person, a place, an organisation, one particular object) is the same when every
    mention names that very individual;
  - a **concept** (a kind of thing, a piece of something, a state, a property) is the same when every mention names the
    same kind, even across documents. Plurals and small wording changes do not make two kinds.

| Label | When |
|---|---|
| VALID | every mention refers to the one thing |
| VALID_ALTERNATIVE | every mention can refer to the one thing, but one needs a reading the text allows without stating it (a short form, a generic word the context pins down) |
| INCORRECT | at least one mention refers to something else. List those mention ids in `outliers`. |
| AMBIGUOUS | the text honestly supports both "same" and "different" for some mention |
| UNJUDGEABLE | the item does not show what a decision needs (an empty sentence and a chunk that does not hold the name) |

Examples:
- **VALID:** a concept joining "proofing basket" and "proofing baskets".
- **VALID_ALTERNATIVE:** an individual joining "Mara Quist" and "Quist", in a letter about Mara Quist alone.
- **INCORRECT:** an individual joining "the North Oven" of one bakery's log with "the North Oven" of
  another bakery's log, where the two logs describe different bakeries.
- **INCORRECT:** a concept joining "crust" (bread) with "crust" (a dry deposit on a tray), where the
  sentences show the two senses.

## Split items (`splits`): "Are these nodes one and the same thing?"

A split item lists several nodes that share a type and a name, each with its mentions. The graph keeps
them apart.

| Label | When |
|---|---|
| VALID | they are different things: keeping them apart is right |
| VALID_ALTERNATIVE | the text does not show they are one thing, though they could be |
| INCORRECT | some of them are one thing: the graph split it. List each group of node ids that are one thing in `together`. |
| AMBIGUOUS | the text honestly supports both |
| UNJUDGEABLE | the item does not show what a decision needs |

Example: **INCORRECT**, `together: [["n1", "n2"]]`, for two "Mara Quist" nodes from the minutes and the
newsletter of one bakery, both naming its head baker.

## Every verdict

- `id`: the item's id, exactly.
- `label`: one of the five labels.
- `reason`: one line saying why.
- `evidence`: a quote copied verbatim from a sentence or chunk the item shows. It must be the words that
  decide the verdict. Only UNJUDGEABLE may leave it empty.
- Judge each item on its own. Do not guess what the graph or any code thinks about it.
