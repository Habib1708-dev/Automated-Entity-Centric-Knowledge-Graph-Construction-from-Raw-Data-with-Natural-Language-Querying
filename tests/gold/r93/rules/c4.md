# C4 record linking: rules for the judge

You judge whether a mention in a text refers to a given record: a row of the structured data. You see:
- the mention's name, its document and sentence, and the full chunk;
- the record's label, key, name, every cell of its row, and its relations to other records one step away;
- other records with the same name, with their relations, so you can tell which of them the text means.

Decide from that text and data alone. Every example below is invented and comes from no dataset you will
judge.

## The question: "Does this mention, read in its chunk, refer to this record?"

A mention refers to a record when a careful reader of the chunk would say the text speaks of that very
row: that object, that person, that event, that piece of that whole. A record that belongs to something
else, as its relations show (a piece of another whole, an event of another object), is not the one meant,
even when its name is the same.

| Label | When |
|---|---|
| VALID | the mention refers to this record |
| VALID_ALTERNATIVE | the text does not pin one record, and this record is one of those it equally means (for example, one row of several that the text cannot tell apart) |
| INCORRECT | the mention refers to another record (name it in the reason when it is listed), or to no record at all (a general kind, or a thing the data has no row for) |
| AMBIGUOUS | the text honestly supports both "this record" and "not this record" |
| UNJUDGEABLE | the item does not show what a decision needs |

Examples:
- **VALID:** in a customer letter about the "Hearth Oven", the mention "the door hinge" is linked to the record
  `Fitting:H-7 "Door Hinge"`, whose relations say `FITTED_TO -> Oven:O-1 (Hearth Oven)`.
- **INCORRECT:** the same mention linked to `Fitting:H-9 "Door Hinge"`, whose relations say
  `FITTED_TO -> Oven:O-4 (Stone Oven)`. The letter is about the Hearth Oven, so the hinge meant is
  another record's. Write that in the reason, for example "the text means H-7, the Hearth Oven's hinge".
- **INCORRECT:** "the inspection" linked to the record of one inspection, in a sentence that speaks of
  inspections in general.
- **VALID:** a mention written exactly like a record's key ("Batch B-1182"), in a chunk about that batch.

## Every verdict

- `id`: the item's id, exactly.
- `label`: one of the five labels.
- `reason`: one line saying why.
- `evidence`: a quote copied verbatim from the chunk or the record that decides it. Only UNJUDGEABLE may
  leave it empty.
- Judge each item on its own. Do not guess what the graph or any code thinks about it.
