## 1. Setting

- Data: 5 CSV files (10 products, 20 suppliers, 88 parts, 64 assemblies, 176 part-supplier rows) and
  10 review files (70 reviews, about 5 500 words). Goal: *supply chain root cause analysis*.
- Preset `quality`: Gemini 3.8 Flash for every role, thinking medium for schema work and low for
  extraction (`presets.yaml`).
- Reviewed plan and reviewed text schema **pinned** (`tests/gold/domain_plan.json`,
  `tests/gold/text_schema.json`); see section 4 for why.
- Judge: Claude Fable 5.1 in the Claude Code session. Gold labelled by the same model family, before the
  extraction output was opened.

## 3. Assessment

**What the system does well**

1. **It does not invent.** Every one of the 126 facts is stated in its review, with a verbatim quote,
   and the judge found no wrong relation, no misread entity, no contradiction. This is the effect of
   two design decisions: extraction into a strict schema, and rejection of any fact whose quote is not
   found in the chunk (2 of 132 facts were rejected for that reason). For a root-cause use, a graph
   that is right about what it says is worth more than one that says more.
2. **The chain the thesis is about works.** "Which suppliers are behind the drawer rails that dresser
   reviews report as defective?" is answered through the text graph, the link to the domain graph and
   the supplier relationship: `Shanghai Metal Corp` and `Korean Metal Works`, both correct. Three of the
   four other questions pass as well.
3. **Structure and provenance are clean.** All 19 validation checks pass; every entity has a source
   chunk, every fact has a chunk id and a quote, every document is linked to its product.
4. **It is cheap.** The whole pipeline on this data set costs about $0.17 with the quality preset, and
   an unchanged prompt costs nothing on a rerun.

**Where it is weak**

1. **Recall: 28 of 96 gold facts are missing (29 %).** 13 of the 28 are in the Helsingborg Dresser
   file, the densest one (28 gold facts from 7 reviews). By kind: 14 `HAS_DEFECT`, 7 `PART_OF`, 4
   `IMPEDES_ASSEMBLY_OF`, 2 `EXHIBITS_FAILURE`, 1 `CAUSES_FAILURE`. Typical misses: "the screws didn't
   fit", "the back panel is basically cardboard", "mismatched rail dimensions", and most "assembly was
   impeded" claims. The extractor keeps the loudest defect of a review and drops the second and third.
2. **Subject choice is inconsistent.** The extractor makes "the holes" or "the surface" the subject
   where the gold expects the product, and uses "table", "dresser", "lamp" as product names. The judge
   accepts these by meaning, but exact match does not, and the question "which products have misaligned
   holes" fails for exactly this reason (it finds one product of six). Entity resolution merges 7 pairs
   but leaves "table" apart from "Gothenburg Table" (`er_accuracy` 0.667).
3. **`PART_OF` dominates the graph: 70 of 126 facts.** They are correct and they are the bridge to the
   suppliers, but 40 of them restate that a praised part belongs to the product. The defect and failure
   facts, which carry the goal's information, are 56.
4. **The LLM's proposals are not stable** (section 4). Without pinning, two runs are not comparable.

**Overall.** Measured on this data, the system is a high-precision, medium-recall extractor with a
working end-to-end chain: what it puts into the graph can be trusted (validated precision 1.00,
evidence verified 100 %), and about seven in ten reference facts reach the graph (validated recall
0.71). The gap between exact-match F1 (0.15) and validated F1 (0.83) shows that most of what looks like
error under string matching is naming, not content. The next improvement is recall in dense reviews,
which is a prompt question, to be measured with a second judge pass against the same gold.

## 4. Findings recorded during this evaluation

- **Schema and plan drift.** A fresh quality run (`84e5eccf`, $0.32, 81 calls, 2 cache hits) proposed a
  different text schema (`OCCURS_IN` reversed from `HAS_DEFECT`, `EXHIBITS`, `CAUSES`, no
  `IMPEDES_ASSEMBLY_OF`, no `Assembly` type) and a different plan (`Component`/`PART_OF` instead of
  `Part`/`CONTAINS`) than the gold was built on, with the same prompts, model and data. Exact-match F1
  fell to 0.063 and all five questions failed on names alone. The graph was therefore rebuilt with the
  reviewed proposals pinned, and every evaluation run now pins them. Schema stability itself is not yet
  measured (open item).
- **Gold corrections.** All 40 are `PART_OF` facts for parts named only in praise sentences. The gold
  rule labelled `PART_OF` only for parts with a defect; the extractor labels every named part. Both are
  supported by the document. Whether the gold rule should be widened is a decision for the next gold
  version, not a change made in this pass.
- **Experiment placement.** `kg eval` without `--preset` logged to `kgbuilder-dev` because `.env` sets
  `KG_PRESET=dev`; the scored run was repeated with `--preset quality`. Rule added to the docs.
- **Cost estimate.** The ≤$0.17 estimate for the first run assumed cache hits that a changed prompt made
  impossible; the actual cost was $0.32. Estimates should quote the no-cache ceiling.

## 5. What this snapshot cannot tell

- Whether a human would agree with the labels or the verdicts (no human labels exist).
- Whether recall of 0.71 is stable: one run, one judge pass, 96 facts.
- Anything about other data sets, other goals or other models.
